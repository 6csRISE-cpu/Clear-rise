# How this system works

Most small home-service businesses run on a treadmill: pay for leads, do the job, repeat. Meanwhile the most valuable asset in the business, the list of people who already paid you once, sits untouched in an invoice app.

This system puts that list to work through **six campaigns**. Each one is a short, written-for-you sequence you can send by text, email or both:

| # | Campaign | What it recovers | When it runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Missed Calls & Quotes | New jobs you were about to lose | Every day *(see the Ghost-Proof Kit included with this system)* |
| 2 | Rebooking | Repeat jobs from past customers | Monthly batch |
| 3 | Cancellation & No-Show Recovery | Jobs that fell off the calendar | Same day as the cancellation |
| 4 | Review Engine | Google reviews, which bring in new calls | After every completed job |
| 5 | Referral Loop | New customers from happy ones | 3–7 days after a 5-star experience |
| 6 | Retention & Maintenance Plans | Predictable recurring revenue | Twice a year, plus at every job |

**You don't have to run all six at once.** The *30-Day Rollout* at the end turns one on per week. Most owners get the first one live in a single afternoon.

> **Fill-Ins used throughout:** `{Business}` · `{You}` · `{Phone}` · `{Link}` (booking link) · `{ReviewLink}` (your Google review link) · `{First}` (customer first name) · `{Job}` (last job) · `{Month}` (month of last service) · `{Offer}` (any genuine offer you choose)

> **Consent reminder:** campaigns 2, 5 and 6 are *promotional*. Send them by text only to customers who agreed to receive texts, and email the rest. See "Texting the Right Way" in the Ghost-Proof Kit.

# Campaign 2 — Rebooking Past Customers

**The idea:** every trade has a natural "come back" interval. Customers mean to rebook, then forget. A well-timed, personal reminder catches them at the right moment.

### Rebooking intervals by trade (starting points, adjust to your experience)

| Trade | Rebook prompt | Interval after last job |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC | Seasonal tune-up (cooling in spring, heating in fall) | 6 months |
| Plumbing | Water heater flush / annual inspection | 12 months |
| House cleaning | Next regular clean / deep clean | 2–4 weeks (regular), 3–6 months (deep) |
| Carpet & upholstery | Re-clean | 6–12 months |
| Lawn & landscaping | Season start / aeration / cleanup | Seasonal |
| Pest control | Quarterly treatment | 3 months |
| Window cleaning | Re-clean | 6 months |
| Gutter cleaning | Spring/fall clean | 6 months |
| Pressure washing | Annual clean | 12 months |
| Electrical | Safety inspection / surge protection / EV charger | 12–24 months |
| Garage doors | Tune-up | 12 months |
| Pool service | Opening / closing | Seasonal |

### How to pull your list (10 minutes, once a month)
Export customers from your invoicing or field-service app (QuickBooks, Jobber, Housecall Pro, Square, etc.) and filter by **last job date** = *your interval* ago, give or take 2 weeks. That's this month's rebooking list. If your app can't filter, sort the spreadsheet by date. The included `Customer-List-Template.csv` shows the columns you need.

### Rebooking sequence (3 touches over 10 days)

**R1 — Day 0 · Text (consented customers) or email**
> Hi {First}, it's {You} from {Business}. It's been about {interval} since we did your {Job} back in {Month}, which is usually when it's due again. Want me to get you on the schedule? I have openings {week}.

**R1 — Email version**
**Subject:** {First}, your {Job} is due

> Hi {First},
>
> It's been about {interval} since we took care of your {Job} in {Month}. That's usually the right time to {benefit: "catch small problems before they turn into expensive ones" / "keep the place feeling fresh" / "keep the warranty valid"}.
>
> If you'd like to get on the schedule, just reply with a day that works, or book here: {Link}.
>
> Thanks for being a customer,
> {You}, {Business}

**R2 — Day 4 · Email · reason to act**
**Subject:** Quick reminder about your {Job}

> Hi {First}, just a quick nudge in case my last note got buried. {One sentence on why it matters now: "Spring is when most AC breakdowns happen, and a tune-up now is much cheaper than an emergency call in July."}
>
> Reply with a day and I'll lock it in. — {You}

**R3 — Day 10 · Text · last call**
> Last one from me, {First}! If you want your {Job} done this {month/season}, reply YES and I'll send you a couple of times. If not, no worries. I'll check back next {season}.

### Rebooking script for phone (top 20 best customers only)
> "Hi {First}, it's {You} from {Business}. You're one of our favorite customers so I wanted to call rather than text. It's about time for your {Job}. I've got {day} or {day} open. Would one of those work?"

# Campaign 3 — Cancellation & No-Show Recovery

**The idea:** a cancellation is not a lost customer, it's a *postponed* one, as long as you rebook within 24 hours. Don't send a guilt trip. Make it easy to say "yes, next week."

**C1 — Immediately after they cancel · Text**
> No problem at all, {First}, thanks for letting me know. Want me to move you to {next available day}, or would you rather pick a time yourself? {Link}

**C2 — Next day if no reply · Text**
> Hi {First}, I still have you on my list for {Job}. I can do {day 1} or {day 2}. Just reply with 1 or 2 and you're set.

**C3 — 7 days later · Email**
**Subject:** Still need that {Job}?

> Hi {First},
>
> I didn't want your {Job} to slip through the cracks after we had to cancel. If it's still on your list, reply with a day that works or grab a time here: {Link}.
>
> If plans changed, no problem. I'll take you off my follow-up list.
>
> {You}

### No-show (you arrived, nobody home)
**N1 — On site · Text**
> Hi {First}, it's {You} from {Business}. I'm outside for your {time} appointment. Are you on your way, or should we reschedule?

**N2 — Same evening · Text**
> Sorry we missed each other today, {First}! Things come up. Want me to put you back on for {day}? Reply with a time that works.

### Reduce cancellations before they happen: the reminder pair
**48 hours before:**
> Hi {First}, reminder that {Business} is scheduled for your {Job} on {day} between {window}. Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule.

**Morning of:**
> Good morning {First}! {You} will be there between {window} today. Reply here if anything changes.

*Confirmations are transactional and tied to an appointment the customer booked.*

# Campaign 4 — The Review Engine

**The idea:** Google reviews drive calls. Most happy customers will leave a review *if you ask at the right moment and make it one tap*. The right moment is right after the job, while they're still happy.

### Step 1 — Get your one-tap review link (5 min, once)
1. Search your business name on Google while logged in to the Google account that manages your Business Profile.
2. Click **"Ask for reviews"** (or "Get more reviews") and copy the link. That's your `{ReviewLink}`.
3. Save it in your phone's text shortcuts / keyboard replacements so you can type ";rev" and it pastes.

### Step 2 — The ask, in person (10 seconds at the end of every job)
> "Hey, if you're happy with how this turned out, it would really help us if you left a quick Google review. I'll text you the link so it takes ten seconds."

*This in-person line doubles the response rate of the text alone. They've said yes to your face.*

### Step 3 — The review text sequence

**V1 — Within 2 hours of finishing · Text**
> Thanks again for having us out today, {First}! If you have 30 seconds, a quick Google review helps a small business like ours more than you'd think: {ReviewLink}. — {You}

**V2 — 3 days later, only if no review · Email**
**Subject:** How did we do, {First}?

> Hi {First},
>
> Thanks again for choosing {Business} for your {Job}. If everything's working the way it should, would you take 30 seconds to share that on Google? {ReviewLink}
>
> And if anything *isn't* right, please reply to this email first. I'd much rather fix it.
>
> {You}

That's it: **two asks, then stop.**

### Step 4 — Reply to every review (2 minutes a week)
**5-star reply:**
> Thank you, {First}! It was great working on your {Job}. We'll see you at your next {service}!

**Critical review reply (calm, short, take it offline):**
> {First}, I'm sorry this wasn't the experience you expected. That's not the standard we hold ourselves to. I'd like to make it right. Please call me directly at {Phone}. — {You}, owner

### What NOT to do
- **Don't offer money, discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews.** Google prohibits incentivized reviews and the FTC treats it as deceptive. Asking *everyone* is fine. Paying for reviews isn't.
- **Don't only ask the happy ones.** Sending unhappy customers to a private form and happy ones to Google is called "review gating" and is against Google's policies. The sequence above asks everyone and invites problems by email, which is allowed.
- **Never write or buy reviews.** Fake reviews can get your profile suspended and carry FTC penalties.

# Campaign 5 — The Referral Loop

**The idea:** a customer who just left you a 5-star review is the warmest marketing channel you'll ever have. Give them an easy, *genuine* reason to mention you to a neighbor.

### Choose your referral reward (pick one, keep it simple)
- **$25–$50 off** their next service for every referral who books (most common)
- **A free add-on** (e.g. filter change, window track cleaning, a free extra room)
- **A donation** to a local school or charity per referral (works well with older customers)

Rewards for *referrals* are fine. Just don't tie them to *reviews*.

**F1 — 3–7 days after a 5-star review or a "thank you!" text · Text/email**
> Thank you so much for the kind words, {First}. It made our week! If you know a neighbor, friend or family member who needs {Trade}, we'd love to help them too. Anyone you send our way gets {new-customer offer}, and you get {reward} when they book. Just have them mention your name.

**F2 — Referral card (print and leave at the job)**
> **{Business}**
> *Know someone who needs {Trade}?*
> Give them this card. They get **{new-customer offer}**, and you get **{reward}** when they book.
> Referred by: ______________
> {Phone} · {Link}

**F3 — Thank-you when a referral books · Text**
> {First}, your neighbor {Referral first name} just booked with us and mentioned you. Thank you! Your {reward} is on your account for next time.

**F4 — Twice a year · Email to all past customers**
**Subject:** A small thank-you (and a favor)

> Hi {First},
>
> We're a small, local business and nearly half our work comes from people like you telling their friends about us. Thank you.
>
> If you know anyone who needs {Trade} this {season}, send them our way. They'll get {new-customer offer}, and you'll get {reward} for every one who books.
>
> {You}, {Business}

# Campaign 6 — Retention & Maintenance Plans

**The idea:** a maintenance plan (also called a membership or service agreement) turns one-time customers into recurring revenue *for your business*: predictable income, scheduled work in slow months, and customers who don't shop around.

### Is a maintenance plan right for your trade?
Strong fit: HVAC, plumbing, pest control, lawn care, pool service, cleaning, window/gutter cleaning, generators, septic.
Possible fit: electrical (annual safety check), garage doors, appliance repair.

### Plan builder (fill this in once)

| Item | Your plan |
|---|---|
| Plan name | {Business} Care Club / Comfort Plan / Home Protection Plan |
| Price | $___ / month or $___ / year *(typical small-shop range: $10–$30/month)* |
| Visits included | e.g. 2 tune-ups / year |
| Member perks | e.g. priority scheduling, 10–15% off repairs, no trip fee |
| Billing | Monthly autopay through your invoicing or field-service app, or annual upfront |
| Cancellation | Any time, no fee (builds trust and actually lowers churn) |

**Pricing sanity check:** the plan should pay for the included visits at your normal rate *before* the discounts. If a tune-up is $129 and you include two, a $19/month plan ($228/year) covers them with a margin for the perks.

### The plan pitch (at the end of every job, 20 seconds)
> "By the way, most of our regular customers are on our {Plan name}. It's ${price} a month and covers your {visits}, plus you jump the line when something breaks and get {discount} off repairs. Want me to send you the details?"

### Plan offer email (to all past customers, twice a year)
**Subject:** Never think about your {system/home} maintenance again

> Hi {First},
>
> A lot of our customers asked for a simple way to stay on top of maintenance without remembering to call, so we created the {Plan name}:
>
> - {visits per year}, scheduled for you automatically
> - Priority scheduling when something goes wrong
> - {discount} off any repair
> - ${price}/month, cancel any time
>
> Reply "PLAN" and I'll set it up, or read more here: {Link}.
>
> {You}

### Keep plan members (retention touches)
- **2 weeks before each included visit:** "Hi {First}, your {visit} is coming up. Here are two times that work…"
- **After each visit:** a short report of what you checked and found, by text or email. *This is the single biggest reason members renew. They see the value.*
- **Annual "thank you" note:** "You've been a member for a year. Here's what we did together: {visits}, {issues caught}."
- **If a member cancels:** "Thanks for being a member, {First}. Could you tell me why you're leaving? If it's price, I can switch you to the annual plan at {lower effective price}."

### Customer anniversary (for everyone, members or not)
> Hi {First}! It's been a year since we first worked on your {Job}. Thanks for trusting {Business}. If there's anything you need this year, I'm a text away.

# AI Prompt Pack

Use these with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or any AI assistant to adapt the templates to *your* business in minutes. Paste the prompt, fill the brackets, and review the output before you send it. **You** are responsible for what goes out, and AI can make things up, so check prices, dates and claims.

**1 — Rewrite any template in my voice**
> I run a small [trade] business called [Business] in [city]. Here's how I usually talk to customers: [paste 2–3 real texts you've sent]. Rewrite the following template so it sounds like me. Keep it under [40] words, no exclamation overload, no marketing language: [paste template].

**2 — Explain my quote in plain English**
> I'm sending a homeowner a quote for [job] at $[price]. The main cost drivers are [list]. Write 2–3 plain-English sentences explaining why it costs what it does, without sounding defensive. Then suggest a lower-cost alternative scope I could offer.

**3 — Seasonal campaign generator**
> Create a 3-message rebooking campaign (1 text, 2 short emails) for a [trade] business for [month] in [region/climate]. Mention one genuine seasonal reason to book now. Texts under 300 characters. Include subject lines. No fake urgency.

**4 — Reply to a review**
> Write a short, warm, specific reply to this Google review for my [trade] business. Mention the job type naturally. Under 50 words. Review: [paste].

**5 — Respond to an unhappy customer**
> A customer is upset because [situation]. Write a calm, non-defensive reply that acknowledges the problem, takes responsibility where appropriate, and invites them to a phone call to fix it. Under 80 words. Don't admit legal fault or promise refunds I haven't offered.

**6 — Build my maintenance plan page**
> Write a simple web page section for a [trade] maintenance plan called [name] at $[price]/month including [visits] and [perks]. Include a 3-question FAQ (cancellation, what's covered, how billing works). Plain language for homeowners.

**7 — Turn a job into a social post**
> Write a short, friendly Facebook/Nextdoor post about a [job] we just did in [neighborhood, no addresses]. Share one useful homeowner tip related to it. Under 80 words. No hashtags spam, max 2.

**8 — Weekly follow-up list from my notes**
> Here are my notes on this week's quotes and jobs: [paste]. Sort them into: (1) quotes to follow up and which touch they're on, (2) customers to ask for a review, (3) cancellations to rebook. Output as a checklist.

**9 — Price objection coach**
> A homeowner said "[objection]" about my $[price] quote for [job]. Give me 3 honest ways to respond by text, each under 40 words: one that explains value, one that offers a smaller scope, one that gracefully lets it go.

**10 — Monthly numbers check-in**
> Here are my numbers for the month: missed calls [x], missed calls texted back [x], replies [x], quotes sent [x], quotes won [x], reviews received [x], rebooked jobs [x]. Tell me which number is the weakest relative to typical small home-service businesses, and give me one specific thing to change next month.
