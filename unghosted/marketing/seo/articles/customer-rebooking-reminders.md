Your customer list is the most valuable marketing asset you have, and most home-service businesses never use it. Past customers already trust you. They mean to rebook. They just forget.

A well-timed reminder catches them at the right moment. Here's when to send one for each trade, and exactly what to say.

## Rebooking intervals by trade

| Trade | What to remind them about | When |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC | Seasonal tune-up | 6 months after last service |
| Plumbing | Water heater flush / inspection | 12 months |
| House cleaning | Next clean / deep clean | 2–4 weeks (regular), 3–6 months (deep) |
| Carpet cleaning | Re-clean | 6–12 months |
| Lawn & landscaping | Season start, aeration, cleanup | Seasonal |
| Pest control | Quarterly treatment | 3 months |
| Window cleaning | Re-clean | 6 months |
| Gutter cleaning | Spring / fall clean | 6 months |
| Pressure washing | Annual clean | 12 months |
| Electrical | Safety inspection | 12–24 months |
| Garage doors | Tune-up | 12 months |

These are starting points. Adjust them to your climate and customers.

## Build this month's list in 10 minutes

Export your customers from your invoicing or field-service app and filter by **last job date** equal to your interval ago, give or take two weeks. That's your rebooking list for the month.

## The 3-touch rebooking sequence

### Touch 1 — Day 0
> Hi {First}, it's {You} from {Business}. It's been about {interval} since we did your {job} back in {month}, which is usually when it's due again. Want me to get you on the schedule?

### Touch 2 — Day 4 (email)
**Subject:** Quick reminder about your {job}

> Just a nudge in case my last note got buried. {One genuine reason it matters now.} Reply with a day and I'll lock it in.

### Touch 3 — Day 10
> Last one from me, {First}! If you want your {job} done this season, reply YES and I'll send a couple of times. If not, no worries.

## A note on consent

Rebooking reminders are promotional. Text them only to customers who agreed to receive texts (a checkbox on your booking form or invoice is the usual way), and email everyone else. *(Practical guidance, not legal advice.)*
