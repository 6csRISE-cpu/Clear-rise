How to Follow Up on a Quote Without Being Pushy (5 Templates for Contractors)

Most quotes aren't lost to a cheaper competitor. They're lost to silence. The homeowner got busy, felt awkward asking a question, or wasn't sure about the price and didn't want to say so.

A short, polite follow-up sequence fixes most of that. Here's the one we recommend: five touches over 21 days, mixing text and email, and each one gives the customer a reason to reply.

The rules

  1. Every touch offers something: an answer, an option, a date. Never just "checking in."
  2. Mix channels. Some people live in email, others only read texts.
  3. Stop the moment they answer, whether it's yes, no or "not now."
  4. "No" is useful. Asking why helps you price better next time.

The 5-touch sequence

Day 1 — Text: "Here's your quote"

Hi {First}, it's {You} from {Business}. Your quote for {Job} is in your email. Any questions at all, just reply here. Happy to walk you through it.

Day 3 — Email: answer the question they're afraid to ask

Subject: Your {Job} quote, plus the question most people ask

The question I get most often is "why does it cost this much?" Here's the honest breakdown: {one or two sentences}. If anything's unclear, or you'd like to see a lower-cost option, just reply.

Day 7 — Text: offer an option

Hi {First}, {You} again. If the quote is more than you planned, I can do a {smaller/phased} version for around {lower price}. Want me to send that over?

This is the most important touch. When price is the hidden objection, a smaller option lets the customer reply without admitting it.

Day 14 — Email: timing

Subject: Holding a spot for {Job}?

Our schedule for {month} is starting to fill. If you'd like it done before {season}, I can hold {date 1} or {date 2}. If the timing isn't right, just let me know and I'll stop nudging.

Only say the schedule is filling if it's true.

Day 21 — Text: close the loop

Hi {First}, I'll close out your {Job} quote this week so I'm not bugging you. If you went another way, totally fine. Would you mind telling me why? One word is plenty (price / timing / went with someone else).

This "break-up" text often gets the most replies of the whole sequence, and some of them are "actually, can we still do it?"

For big-ticket quotes

For quotes over about $3,000, stretch the sequence to 30 days. Add a financing or payment-plan email around day 10 and a real phone call at day 30.

How to make it automatic

Many field-service apps can send quote follow-ups automatically. Search their help center for "quote follow-up" or "estimate follow-up." If yours can't, put reminders on your calendar for days 1, 3, 7, 14 and 21. Five minutes a week keeps it running.

Track one number

Measure your quote close rate (quotes won ÷ quotes sent) for a month before you start and a month after. That's how you'll know it's working.
