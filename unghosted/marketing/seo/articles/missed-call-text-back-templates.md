You're under a sink, on a roof or driving between jobs, and the phone rings. You can't answer. By the time you call back, the homeowner has booked someone else.

Call-tracking studies of home-service businesses put the share of unanswered calls somewhere between **14% and 27%**. You'll never answer every call. But you *can* reply to every missed call within five minutes, and a text is the fastest way to do it.

Below are seven missed-call text-back templates you can copy today, plus how to send them automatically.

## Why a text works better than a callback

- **It's instant.** A text sent automatically within a minute beats a callback two hours later.
- **It's easy to answer.** Homeowners who are at work can reply quietly.
- **It stops the next call.** When a caller gets a personal reply, most of them stop dialing your competitors.

## The 7 templates

Replace `{You}`, `{Business}` and `{First}` with your details.

### 1. Business hours (start here)
> Hi, this is {You} with {Business}. Sorry I missed your call, I'm on a job right now. What can I help you with? Reply here and I'll get back to you within the hour.

### 2. After hours
> Hi, it's {You} at {Business}. We're closed for the night, but I saw your call. Reply with what you need and the best time to reach you, and I'll call first thing in the morning. If it's an emergency, reply URGENT.

### 3. Weekend
> Thanks for calling {Business}! We're off for the weekend but checking messages. Tell me what's going on and I'll get you on the schedule.

### 4. Emergency trades (plumbing, HVAC, electrical)
> This is {Business}, sorry we missed you. If you have water leaking, no heat/AC or sparking, reply NOW and we'll call you right back. Otherwise reply with a quick description and we'll get you scheduled.

### 5. With a booking link
> Hi, {You} from {Business} here. Missed your call! Fastest way to get on the schedule is here: {Link}. Or just reply with what you need.

### 6. Second touch (2–3 hours later, no reply)
> Just checking you got sorted, {First}. Still happy to help with that if you need it. I have an opening tomorrow morning.

### 7. Final touch (next day)
> No worries if you found someone! If you still need help, we're here. Just reply to this text.

Three touches, then stop. More than that feels pushy.

## Record a voicemail greeting that sets up the text

> "You've reached {Business}, this is {You}. I'm probably on a job. You'll get a text from this number in a few minutes. Just reply to it, it's the fastest way to reach me."

Callers who *expect* a text don't mistake it for spam.

## How to set up automatic missed-call text-back

Most tools have this built in. Search your app's help center for **"missed call text back"** or **"auto-reply."**

| You use… | Where to look |
|---|---|
| A field-service app (Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Workiz…) | Communications / phone settings or automations |
| A business phone app (Quo/OpenPhone, RingCentral, Grasshopper…) | Auto-replies or missed-call settings |
| Google Voice | No automatic text-back; send manually using a saved reply |
| A regular cell phone | iPhone "Respond with Text" or Android quick replies, sent manually |

## Stay on the right side of the rules

Replying to someone who just called *you* about their own request is conversational. Promotional texts (offers, seasonal reminders) are different and need the customer's prior written consent. Always honor STOP. U.S. business texting apps also require a one-time "10DLC" registration, and your provider walks you through it. *(Practical guidance, not legal advice.)*
