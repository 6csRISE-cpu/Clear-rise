# Community Answer Bank

**Q: "How do I get more Google reviews?"**
> Ask out loud at the end of every job, with the same words every time ("would you mind leaving us an honest Google review? I'll text you the link"), then text the one-tap review link within 2 hours. One reminder 3 days later, then stop. Saying it in person makes a big difference. Don't offer anything for reviews, don't only ask the happy ones, and don't say "if you're happy…". Google prohibits all of it.

**Q: "Customers ghost me after I send a quote."**
> Most quotes die to silence, not price. Try 5 touches over 3 weeks, each one giving something: Day 1 "it's in your inbox, questions?"; Day 3 explain the price in 2 sentences; Day 7 offer a smaller/cheaper scope; Day 14 offer two specific dates; Day 21 "I'll close this out so I'm not bugging you. Mind telling me why you passed?" That last one gets the most replies by far.

**Q: "I miss calls while I'm on jobs. Should I hire an answering service?"**
> Before paying for one, set up missed-call text-back (most field-service and business phone apps have it). A text within 5 minutes that says "sorry I missed you, I'm on a job, what do you need?" wins back a lot of those callers. Change your voicemail greeting to tell people to expect the text. If you're still losing a lot after that, an answering service makes sense.

**Q: "How do I get repeat customers / what do I do in the slow season?"**
> Export your customer list and filter by last job date: everyone due for the same service again (6 months for HVAC tune-ups, 12 for water heater flushes, 3 for pest, etc.). That's a marketing message, so text only the customers who gave written consent to marketing texts, and email the rest: "It's been about a year since we did your X, which is usually when it's due. Want me to get you on the schedule?" It's the cheapest marketing you'll ever do.

**Q: "Should I offer a maintenance plan? How do I price it?"**
> Price it so the included visits roughly pay for themselves: two $129 tune-ups ≈ $258/yr ≈ $19–21/mo. Add priority scheduling and 10–15% off repairs. Make cancellation free and easy, because it makes joining feel safe. Pitch it in one sentence at the end of every job. Send members a short report after every visit; that's what makes them renew.

**Q: "Is it legal to text my customers?"**
> Think in four buckets: (1) replying to someone who contacted you, which is fine if you stay on their request; (2) service messages about a booked job or a quote they asked for, which need their OK to text, collected at booking; (3) promotional texts, which need written consent first; (4) automated marketing blasts, which need written consent, a registered (10DLC) number and automatic opt-outs. Quiet hours 8am–8pm, and any "stop" means stop immediately. Not legal advice. Talk to a TCPA attorney if you text at volume.
