# Start here (15 minutes)

You don't need more leads before you've followed up on the ones you already have. A good share of the calls a home-service business gets go unanswered. Industry call-tracking studies put it somewhere between 1 in 7 and 1 in 4. And most homeowners who get a quote and don't hear back simply hire whoever followed up first.

This kit fixes two leaks:

1. **Missed calls.** Someone called, nobody picked up, and they're dialing your competitor.
2. **Quiet quotes.** You sent a price, they said "let me think about it," and you never heard from them again.

> **Fill-Ins.** Replace these everywhere:
> `{Business}` your company name · `{You}` your first name · `{Phone}` your callback number · `{Link}` your booking link or website (optional) · `{Trade}` what you do (e.g. "AC repair", "house cleaning")

# Part 1 — Missed-Call Text-Back

Send within **5 minutes** of the missed call, faster if you can. Speed matters more than wording. Most field-service apps (Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Workiz and others) and most business phone apps (Google Voice, OpenPhone/Quo, RingCentral, Grasshopper) have an automatic missed-call text setting. If yours doesn't, set your phone's "busy" auto-reply and send by hand.

> **Before you automate:** replying to someone who *just called you* about their own request is generally treated as a conversational, customer-initiated message. Marketing texts are different and need written consent. See *Part 4: Texting the Right Way*.

### Text #1 — Standard (business hours) ★ start with this one
> Hi, this is {You} with {Business}. Sorry I missed your call, I'm on a job right now. What can I help you with? Reply here and I'll get back to you within the hour.

### Text #2 — After hours
> Hi, it's {You} at {Business}. We're closed for the night, but I saw your call. Reply with what you need and the best time to reach you, and I'll call first thing in the morning. If it's an emergency, reply URGENT.

### Text #3 — Weekend
> Thanks for calling {Business}! We're off for the weekend but checking messages. Tell me what's going on and I'll get you on the schedule. Monday mornings fill up fast, so I'll hold a spot if you reply today.

### Text #4 — Emergency trades (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, restoration)
> This is {Business}, sorry we missed you. If you have water leaking, no heat/AC, sparking or a gas smell, reply NOW and we'll call you right back. For gas smells, leave the house and call your gas company first. Otherwise reply with a quick description and we'll get you scheduled.

### Text #5 — Booking-link version
> Hi, {You} from {Business} here. Missed your call! Fastest way to get on the schedule is here: {Link}. Or just reply with what you need and I'll text you back.

### Text #6 — Second touch (if no reply after 2–3 hours)
> Just checking you got sorted, {First}. Still happy to help with that if you need it. I have an opening {tomorrow morning / Thursday afternoon}.

> **Why the second touch works:** people call three companies at once. The first one to *actually talk to them* usually wins. The specific opening makes it easy to say yes.

### Text #7 — Final touch (next day, no reply)
> No worries if you found someone, {First}! If you still need {Trade}, we're here. Just reply to this text. — {You}, {Business}

That's the last one. Three touches, then stop. More than that starts to feel pushy.

### Voicemail greeting (record this — 20 seconds)
> "You've reached {Business}, this is {You}. I'm probably on a job. You'll get a text from this number in the next few minutes. Just reply to it with what you need, it's the fastest way to reach me. If it's an emergency, say 'emergency' in your message and I'll call right back."

This tells callers to *expect* the text, so they don't ignore it as spam.

### When they reply: the 3-question qualifier
Keep it to three questions so it doesn't feel like a form:
1. "What's going on?" (the problem)
2. "What's the address or zip?" (are they in your area)
3. "When works best for you, mornings or afternoons?" (move to booking)

Then: "Perfect. I can do {day} between {time window}. Want me to lock that in?"

# Part 2 — The Quote Follow-Up Sequence

Most quotes aren't lost to a cheaper competitor. They're lost to **silence**. The homeowner got busy, forgot, or felt awkward asking a question. This 5-touch sequence over 21 days is polite, useful and easy to run.

**Rules that make it work**
- Every touch gives them something: an answer, an option, a reason. Never just "checking in."
- Mix text and email. Some people live in one, not the other.
- Stop the moment they say yes, no, or "not now."
- "No" is a fine answer. Ask why (Touch 5) and you'll learn how to price better.

### Touch 1 — Day 0/1 · Text · "Here's your quote"
> Hi {First}, it's {You} from {Business}. Your quote for {Job} is in your email. Any questions at all, just reply here. Happy to walk you through it.

### Touch 2 — Day 3 · Email · "The question most people ask"
**Subject:** Your {Job} quote, plus the question most people ask

> Hi {First},
>
> Following up on the {Job} quote I sent over on {date}.
>
> The question I get most often is *"why does it cost this much?"* Here's the honest breakdown: {one or two sentences, e.g. "about half is the unit itself, the rest is the labor to install it to code and haul the old one away"}.
>
> If anything in the quote is unclear, or you want to see a lower-cost option, just reply and I'll put one together.
>
> Thanks,
> {You}
> {Business} · {Phone}

### Touch 3 — Day 7 · Text · "Options"
> Hi {First}, {You} again. If the {Job} quote is more than you planned, I can do a {smaller scope / phased / basic} version for around {lower price}. Want me to send that over?

> **Why it works:** when price is the silent objection, giving them a smaller option lets them reply without admitting it.

### Touch 4 — Day 14 · Email · "Schedule / timing"
**Subject:** Holding a spot for {Job}?

> Hi {First},
>
> Quick one. Our schedule for {month} is starting to fill. If you'd like the {Job} done before {season/holiday/the hot weather}, I can hold {date option 1} or {date option 2} for you.
>
> If the timing isn't right, no problem at all. Just let me know and I'll stop nudging.
>
> {You}

*Only say the schedule is filling if it's true. Honesty keeps your reputation.*

### Touch 5 — Day 21 · Text · "Closing the loop"
> Hi {First}, I'll close out your {Job} quote this week so I'm not bugging you. If you went another way, totally fine. Would you mind telling me why? One word is plenty (price / timing / went with someone else). It helps me a lot. Thanks either way! — {You}

This "break-up" message often gets the most replies of the whole sequence, and a surprising number are "Actually, can we still do it?"

### Big-ticket version (quotes over ~$3,000)
Big jobs take longer to decide on. Add these two touches and stretch the schedule to 30 days:
- **Day 10 · Email — "Financing / payment options":** "Some customers prefer to spread the cost. We offer {financing partner / 2–3 payments}. Here's how it works: …"
- **Day 30 · Phone call:** a real call. "Hi {First}, it's {You} from {Business}. Calling about the {Job} quote. No pressure, I just wanted to see whether you had any questions I could answer."

### If they reply "too expensive"
> Totally fair, thanks for being straight with me. Can I ask what you were hoping to spend? Sometimes I can change the scope, the materials or the timing to get closer. If not, no hard feelings.

### If they reply "we went with someone else"
> Thanks for letting me know, {First}. I appreciate it. If anything comes up down the road, I'm a text away. Good luck with the project!

*(Then add them to your rebooking list. See the Unghosted System.)*

# Part 3 — Phone Scripts

### Calling back a missed caller
> "Hi, is this {First}? This is {You} from {Business}. You called us a little while ago and I'm sorry I missed you. I was on a job. What's going on?"

Then use the 3-question qualifier from Part 1.

### Quote follow-up call (Day 30 or big tickets)
> "Hi {First}, it's {You} from {Business}. I'm calling about the {Job} quote. I'm not calling to pressure you, I just know quotes can be confusing. Was there anything in it you'd like me to explain?"
>
> *(Listen. Don't fill silence.)*
>
> If interested: "Great, the earliest I can get you in is {date}. Does that work?"
> If not now: "No problem. Would it help if I checked back in {a month / spring}?"
> If no: "Totally understand. Do you mind if I ask what made the decision? It helps me improve."

# Part 4 — Texting the Right Way (Read This Once)

*This is practical guidance, not legal advice. Rules vary by country and state, so check with your phone provider or an advisor if you're unsure.*

**In the U.S., the simple version:**
1. **Replying to someone who contacted you** (a missed call, a quote request, a scheduled job) is conversational. Keep it about *their* request.
2. **Promotional texts** (seasonal offers, discounts, "we have openings") need the customer's **prior written consent**. A checkbox on your booking form or a line on your invoice/estimate is the usual way. Sample consent wording:
   > ☐ Yes, {Business} may text me appointment reminders, follow-ups and occasional offers. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out at any time.
3. **Always honor STOP** immediately, and keep a note of who opted out.
4. **Business texting apps** in the U.S. require "10DLC" registration of your business number. Your texting provider walks you through it. It's a one-time form.
5. **Quiet hours:** don't send promotional texts before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in the customer's time zone. Some states are stricter.
6. **Emails** need your business address and an unsubscribe option if they're promotional. Most email tools add these automatically.

Following these rules isn't just about avoiding fines. It keeps your number off spam lists, so your texts actually get delivered.
